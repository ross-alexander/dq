\section{A Guide to Ranking}

\begin{multicols}{2}

At times the jigsaw puzzle called Ranking appears difficult and
unclear, however it’s generally worth the effort. Section 5 of the
Rules has the specifics but here is an informal look at it.

\subsection{Ill-gotten Gains}

It was fun but what do you get out of it?  Ranking (or training)
requires three things: Experience Points (EP), time and money.  EP is
awarded by your GM and is a measure of how much your character can
learn. You might also gain cash, magical or mundane items and
sometimes changes to your character.

\subsection{Experience Points}

The main way to gain EP after character generation is by role-playing
with a recognised GM. You will gain EP on adventure for good
roleplaying, for using your abilities sensibly, and for entertainment.
Divide this EP by your Racial Experience Modifier. Add this to your
unspent EP to give your available EP for Ranking.

Most other races learn less from adventuring than humans, so they gain
less EP. On your standard character sheet there is a box marked RM
(Racial Multiplier), that will have the EP multiplier for your race in
it.  Divide all EP from your adventure by this number. This means that
most non-humans will lose EP.  However some non-humans races gain EP
discounts for specific abilities. These are detailed in the Rules,
Section 2.2.

\subsection{Campaign and Training Time}

Time in the campaign passes at the same rate as time in the real
world, i.e. one game year equals one real year. The campaign, however,
uses a different calendar. The dates between the two are synchronised
seasonally at the Guild meetings. Your character can train anytime
they are not out on adventure or travelling (though you might like to
give them occasional holidays). Generally this means from the day
after the end of your last adventure to the day before your next
adventure starts.

If your character’s time is devoted entirely to training, you may
train in two related abilities per day, one in the morning and one in
the afternoon. You cannot rank non-magical abilities at the same time
as magical abilities and you may not halve the training time by
ranking one ability fulltime. The non-magical abilities are Skills,
Weapons, Adventuring Skills and Characteristics and the magical
abilities are Spells, Rituals, and Talents.

\subsection{Ranking Record}

All characters must have a permanent record of their progression and
training. This can be a notebook or a printout (many players use
spreadsheets to keep track of ranking).  Ranking (training) should be
worked before the first night of play in each session. This record
needs to be available for your GM to check, at which point they will
assist you in confirming that your ranking is correct, and then sign
it off.

Clarity is the essence of all good ranking.  Numerical accuracy also
assists. We suggest that you use a column format, with headings of
Ability, Current Rank, New Rank/s being trained, EP spent, time taken,
and silver pennies spent. See table ‘Example Ranking’.  The reason why
we stress the importance of this is that your GM has to check all
players ranking at the beginning of the session, and on the first
night everyone wants to get roleplaying as soon as possible. Anything
which makes ranking easier to check is appreciated by all.

\subsection{What You Get}

Firstly, the GM will give you a slip of paper with the following
information:

\begin{itemize}

\item The total EP gained by the character on the adventure,
\item The start and end dates of the
adventure (both game and real time),

\item Cash gained (usually net after tax but before the value of items
  is deducted),

\item Any special training opportunities, e.g.  adventuring skills.
\end{itemize}

Add the EP gained directly into your ranking records. If you have
received any physical gains other than cash (items, special abilities
etc), your GM will provide you with written descriptions.

NB. If you don’t have a write-ups for an item, your character is
deemed not to have the item available during the game.

\subsection{What to Do with it}

All adventurers can learn any skill, any weapon they have the minimum
physical characteristics for, and any magic within their college.
Learning costs EP, Time and Money.  There are 3 major types of
abilities: Skills, Weapons and Spells and 5 lesser ones: Rituals,
Talents, Names, Adventuring Skills and Characteristics.

If you don’t have enough time in one block to finish a rank in an
ability you may split the time up but all training for a given rank
must be completed within 6 months of starting.

\subsection{Skills (and languages)}

All skills are unknown (or unranked) initially.  To learn a skill
takes 8 weeks initially and this gives you Rank 0 (except Languages,
which only take 1 week to learn Rank 0). Each subsequent rank takes 1
× (new rank) weeks (e.g. to go to Rank 7 from Rank 6 will take 7
weeks). Rank 10 is the maximum Rank achievable in all Skills. The EP
cost for ranking Skills is listed on Table 87.8.

Some skills have minimum Characteristic requirements to Rank or impose
EP penalties (or discounts) for exceptional Characteristics.  You will
get an EP discount of 10\% if you have a trainer. Guild organised
trainers cost 150 sp per week of training, except for Rank 0, which
costs 150 sp. If no training assistance is available at all, a 25\% EP
penalty is applied, except for Languages, which may not be ranked
without a trainer.  Some Skills and Languages may have additional EP
discounts as detailed in the Rules for each skill.

Achieving Ranks 8, 9 and 10 is difficult. You must find and complete a
special task (master work) related to your skill for each of these
ranks. A GM must approve this.

\subsection{Weapons}

All weapons are unranked initially. Learning Rank 0 takes 1 week. Each
subsequent Rank takes 2 × (new Rank) weeks. Weapons have individual
maximum Ranks between 2 and 10.  EP cost for ranking Weapons is listed
on Table 87.8.

All weapons require minimum PS and MD characteristics. If your
character does not fulfil both requirements, they may not rank the
weapon.

There are no EP discounts for weapons, nor may you train without a
trainer. Training at the Guild costs 10 × (new rank squared) sp, or 10
sp for Rank 0.

\subsection{Adventuring Skills}

Adventuring Skills are skills that are used every day by adventurers
to survive and thus are being continually honed. These skills include
Climbing, Flying, Horsemanship (riding), Stealth and
Swimming. Adventurers start off with Rank 0 or more in all these
skills as part of character generation (unless specifically told
otherwise).

If you have extensively used an adventuring skill while on adventure
you may increase this skill one rank without any time
requirements. Otherwise Adventuring Skills are have the same time and
money costs as Skills. The EP costs for adventuring skills are
detailed in Table 87.8 (as part of Skills).  There are no special
requirements for Ranks 8, 9 or 10 and Rank 10 is the maximum rank
achievable in all adventuring skills.

\subsection{Characteristics}

Characteristics are generated as part of Character Generation. PS, MD,
AG, MA, WP, EN and FT may not be increased more than 5 points. PB may
not be increased and PC may be increased until it reaches Racial
Maximum. No characteristic may go over Racial Maximum.

If you have lost characteristics during adventure you may buy them
back to the level they started the adventure at (unless specifically
told otherwise).

You may also increase characteristics once after each adventure. The
EP costs are: PC: 1000 for the first point, 750 per point thereafter

\subsection{Spells}

If you are an Adept (i.e. cast spells) you can rank your spells. Each
spell has an Experience Multiplier (EM). This number is multiplied by
your new rank, to give a total EP cost. It takes (new Rank) days to
rank a spell and does not require a trainer.

General Knowledge spells may have EP discount for Adepts with MA >
15. This discount is (MA - 15) × 5\%.

All Special Knowledge spells are unranked initially. Learning Rank 0
takes 1 + (EM / 100 rounded up) weeks, no EP, but requires a
trainer. The training costs for learning spells are detailed in the
Magical Training section of this Guide.

All spells must be cast at least 5 times before achieving a new rank.
This may be done on adventure, but if not, the cast chances must be
rolled with a GM as the spells may backfire.

Rank 20 is the maximum rank achievable with any spell. You cannot have
more spells and rituals below rank 6 than your MA.

\subsection{Rituals}

If you are an Adept you can rank your rituals.  Each ritual has an EM,
which is multiplied by your new rank, to give a total EP cost. It
takes (Rank to be achieved) weeks to rank a ritual and does not
require a trainer.  General Knowledge Rituals may have EP discount for
Adepts with MA > 15. This discount is (MA - 15) × 5\%.

All special knowledge Rituals are unranked initially. Learning Rank 0
takes 1 + (EM / 100 rounded up) weeks, no EP, but requires a
trainer. The training costs for learning Rituals are detailed in
Section 4.1 of this Guide.

Rituals must be performed at least once before achieving a new
rank. It is not necessary to use material components, but in that case
the ritual will fail (and may still back fire). Rank 20 is the maximum
rank achievable with any ritual.

\subsection{Talents}

For each week adventuring you can rank each of your talents once. This
entitlement does not accrue, and if you do not rank them before the
next adventure then it is lost.  Each Talent has an EM, which is
multiplied by your new rank, to give a total EP cost. No training time
is required to rank Talents.  There are no MA discounts for ranking
Talents.

\subsection{Names}

All adventurers can learn Names but only Namers can gain any rank
beyond 0. Names can be acquired from someone who knows the name or by
Detect Aura and then studied for one day to reach rank 0 (or for one
week in the case of an individual true name).  Ranking a Name does not
require EP, takes rank × weeks to gain rank, and can be done in
addition to any other ranking; It can also replace ranking any single
magical or non-magical ability; i.e. Namers may rank 1 name while
learning two related abilities, 2 names with one ability or, if doing
no other ranking, 3 names at once.
