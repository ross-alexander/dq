\section{Coinage \& Measures}

\subsection{Coinage}

\begin{multicols}{2}
  
The value of a coin is determined by its weight and metal of which it is made.

\smallskip

\begin{cointblr}{
    colspec={XQ[r,4em]Q[r,4em]},
    rowsep=0.0pt,
    stretch=0.9,
    hlines={lightgray},
    }
Name                   & Weight        &  Conv. \\
Copper farthing (cf)   & 1/5 oz        & \\
Silver penny (sp)      & 1/20 oz       & 4 cf \\
Gold shilling (gs)     & 1/20 oz       & 12 sp \\
Truesilver guinea (tg) & 1/10 oz       & 21 gs 252sp \\
\end{cointblr}

\smallskip

NB. 320 sp weigh 1 pound

The value of larger amounts is determined by its weight and metal of
which it is made.

\smallskip

\begin{cointblr}{
    colspec={XQ[r,4em]Q[r,4em]},
    rowsep=0.0pt,
    stretch=0.9,
    hlines={lightgray},
    }
Name                     & Weight   & Conv. \\
Silver Ingot             & 5 lb     & 1,600 sp \\
Gold Ingot               & 5 lb     & 19,200 sp \\
Platinum Ingot           & 5 lb     & 20,800 sp \\
Half Truesilver Ingot    & 1/2 lb   & 20,160 sp \\
Truesilver Ingot         & 1 lb     & 40,320 sp \\
\end{cointblr}

Some ingots come in long strips with indentations to snap them at ¼
and ½ of the ingot value. The values and weights of these coins
correspond to those of the Silver Penny.

\end{multicols}

\subsection{Useful Measurements}

\begin{multicols}{2}
  \raggedcolumns
  
In DQ we use imperial measurement system partly because the original
DQ rules used it, but mostly these days to remind players they are not
in the modern world. Here are many of the commonly used measurements.
